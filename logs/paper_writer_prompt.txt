## 2. Research Question & Motivation

The primary joint hypothesis is that adding exactly `If you are uncertain, answer exactly I do not know.` lowers Qwen3-8B incorrect-answer frequency by at least 0.10 while reducing correct-answer frequency by at most 0.03. Identical secondary tests target the specified DeepSeek and Moonlight checkpoints. A paired design distinguishes selective avoidance of errors from indiscriminately answering less.

### Literature review summary

The gathered SimpleQA paper motivates the correct/incorrect/not-attempted decomposition and cautions about benchmark label error. AbstentionBench motivates checkpoint-specific tests and distinguishes answerable factual questions from inherently unanswerable questions. R-Tuning and Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know concern training or confidence methods outside this sole-suffix intervention. SimpleQA Verified motivates label-noise and grader-domain-shift caveats; it is not substituted for the mandated dataset. This is a narrow replication-style checkpoint study, not a new abstention algorithm. See [literature_review.md](literature_review.md) and [planning.md](planning.md).

### Statistical analysis and gates

Each item’s three binary seed outcomes are averaged before computing means/SEs and paired intervention-minus-baseline effects. Percentile CIs use 10,000 bootstrap resamples of 400 item clusters, seed 20261020; linear SEs use the sample SD across item averages divided by sqrt(400). Ratios use bootstrap SEs. Synthetic calibration resamples 50 question clusters, preserving each triple. Primary support requires Δincorrect ≤ −0.10, two-sided 95% CI upper bound <0, and one-sided 95% lower bound for Δcorrect ≥ −0.03.

Secondary tests use paired one-sided t tests for incorrect decrease (margin 0) and correct retention (margin −0.03), joint intersection-union p=max(component p values), with Holm adjustment over the two checkpoints. The observed effect threshold and bootstrap gates still apply. The fixed sample may be underpowered for retention; failure to pass does not establish equivalence, absence of risk or no effect.

Missing labels contribute to the explicit unscored category. Invalid and truncated are overlapping diagnostic flags, not additional mutually exclusive outcome classes. Known-class frequencies are observed contributions over all planned items, not missing-at-random imputations. Adversarial bounds put unknown treatment outcomes at 0 and baseline at 1 for the minimum effect, reversing those assignments for the maximum. Both bound endpoints have bootstrap intervals in `results/analysis.json`.

## 5. Analysis & Discussion

The primary hypothesis is inconclusive: excessive unscored judge outputs prevent a valid factuality conclusion, and conservative missing-label bounds do not establish correct-answer retention. Azure dual grading is also incomplete.

The observed-label Qwen contrast meets the numerical error/retention rule, but this is not a supported result: 187/2400 Qwen responses are unscored, above the preregistered 2% suppression threshold. There are 78/2400 unscored DeepSeek responses and 112/2400 unscored Moonlight responses. These are exact inventory counts; condition-specific rate SEs/CIs are reported above. Five missing labels arise from DeepSeek no-final parsing; the other 372 arise from malformed judge replies. The strict parser is not relaxed after seeing these results. Malformed replies include repeated labels such as `B B`, conflicting text such as `C CORRECT`, and invented dialogue continuations. These are formatting failures under the fixed protocol, not known factual misjudgments. The key comparison is error reduction alongside retained correct answers. A larger not-attempted fraction and smaller incorrect fraction can arise solely through coverage reduction; neither demonstrates improved stored knowledge. Narrow retention intervals and judge agreement matter to the joint criterion. Any apparent differences between checkpoints are descriptive for these weights and protocols and cannot be attributed causally to distillation or generalized to model families.

The study records infrastructure problems separately from model-generated failures. The pre-main package-resolution issue and external GPU contention are documented in `results/runtime/infrastructure_events.json`. Raw responses permit inspection of judge disagreement once Azure becomes available; no disagreement rate is estimated from a single judge. Deterministically selected truncation/no-final examples are in `results/diagnostic_cases.json`. Exploratory bounds treating truncated as well as unscored outputs as unknown are in `results/truncation_sensitivity.json`; these do not replace the preregistered analysis.

## 6. Limitations

- Required Azure calibration and dual grading are incomplete because daily headroom could not be verified. Neither absence of a ledger nor zero calls establishes judge calibration.
- Synthetic controls are much simpler than natural partial answers, hedges, aliases and hallucinations. Their agreement is not human-ground-truth accuracy; all local findings remain provisional.
- Original SimpleQA gold labels can contain errors and semantic redundancy; exact ID uniqueness does not eliminate these issues or training contamination.
- Narrow retention margins may have insufficient power at 400 items. Nonsignificance does not establish no effect or equivalence.
- External GPU contention and the documented reduced reservations affect operational reproducibility. Native sampling settings can differ between checkpoints, though each paired contrast holds them fixed.
- DeepSeek reasoning consumes the total budget; missing final boundaries and truncations are part of this finite-budget protocol, not necessarily factual ignorance.
- The local Qwen judge shares ancestry with the primary Qwen subject. No intervention tuning was performed, but correlated errors remain possible.
- Inspect artifacts are explicitly post-hoc replay logs from real cached outputs, not fresh inference or contemporaneous collection logs. Replay’s default completion-level SEs do not replace the reported item-cluster uncertainty.

## 7. Conclusions & Next Steps

## Motivation & Novelty Assessment
### Why This Research Matters
Factual assistants can reduce wrong answers simply by answering less. Users and evaluators need a paired measurement of errors and retained correct answers to distinguish useful selective answering from indiscriminate abstention.
### Gap in Existing Work
The gathered SimpleQA and abstention literature does not establish the fixed 0.10 error-reduction / 0.03 correct-retention criterion for these three pinned checkpoints. Historical benchmark scores and synthetic judge controls are not natural-response calibration.
### Our Novel Contribution
A narrowly scoped, preregistered replication-style checkpoint comparison with the sole fixed suffix, paired questions, repeated seeds, and explicit judge/missingness gates. No claim of a new abstention method or exhaustive novelty search.
### Experiment Justification
1. Primary Qwen3 contrast directly tests the joint hypothesis.
2. Identical DeepSeek and Moonlight contrasts test checkpoint-specific transfer, Holm adjusted.
3. Gold/negation/abstention controls and dual grading test measurement implementation and sensitivity, without establishing human-ground-truth validity.

### SimpleQA full-text chunk notes (Wei et al., 2024; arXiv:2411.04368v1)
- Chunk 1, pp. 1–3: 4,326 fact-seeking questions, adversarial collection against GPT-4 variants, two independent annotators, intended timeless answers available by December 31, 2023. Short-form parametric factuality is the target; no evidence that results generalize to long-form answers.
- Chunk 2, pp. 4–6: third-annotator audit of 1,000 questions estimated about 3% benchmark error; distinguish reference-label problems from judge mistakes. Rubric: correct contains gold without contradiction; incorrect includes hedged contradictions; not attempted omits required answer without contradiction. Overall correct and correct-given-attempted must be reported separately. F-score still incentivizes some guessing.
- Chunk 3, pp. 7–9: published GPT-4o-mini rates 8.6% correct / 90.5% incorrect / 0.9% not attempted; Claude-3.5-sonnet 28.9% / 36.1% / 35.0%. These historical results illustrate coverage tradeoffs, not expected scores for our checkpoints. Stated confidence is overconfident; repeated-answer frequency correlates with accuracy. References identify Kadavath et al. as calibration foundation.
- Chunk 4, pp. 10–12: full grader examples allow aliases, minor typos, numeric rounding and inferred units. Partial answers require semantic grading: an incomplete definitive answer and an explicitly acknowledged partial answer can differ. Never replace rubric with exact string matching. A/B/C output maps to CORRECT/INCORRECT/NOT_ATTEMPTED.
- Chunk 5, p. 13: F = 2c/(2c+2i+a), where a is not-attempted count and all cases are scored. Confidence elicitation is a distinct intervention and is excluded from the fixed-suffix experiment.

## Key papers

**1. Wei, Jason; Nguyen, Karina; Chung, Hyung Won; Jiao, Yunxin Joy; Papay, Spencer; Glaese, Amelia; Schulman, John; Fedus, William (2024). Measuring short-form factuality in large language models.** arXiv:2411.04368v1. Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04368`. Local: `papers/2411.04368_simpleqa.pdf`.

The central contribution is a challenging 4,326-item factuality benchmark with correct/incorrect/not-attempted grading. Historical GPT/Claude baselines differ substantially in willingness to answer. Method, numerical examples and caveats are documented chunk by chunk above. Official code: `https://github.com/openai/simple-evals` (cloned). The paper motivates our estimands and rubric, but provides no evidence for the exact Qwen3 0.10/0.03 joint threshold. Its informal judge spot check is not calibration of our two judges.

**2. Kirichenko, Polina; Ibrahim, Mark; Chaudhuri, Kamalika; Bell, Samuel J. (2025). AbstentionBench: Reasoning LLMs Fail on Unanswerable Questions.** arXiv:2506.09038v1. Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09038`. Local: `papers/2506.09038_abstentionbench.pdf`.

Curates 20 datasets covering six scenarios (unknown answers, false premises, stale information, subjectivity, underspecified context/intent), including modified GSM8K/GPQA/MMLU and established QA datasets. Evaluates 20 LLMs and post-training/reasoning variants, with abstention precision/recall/F1 and a separate correctness judge. A Llama-3.1-8B abstention judge achieved 88% agreement on their manually annotated sample (p. 5). The abstract reports reasoning fine-tuning degrading abstention by 24% on average; sections 4.3–4.4 discuss reasoning and prompting. Stronger prompting can help, but no universal model-size advantage appears. This is evidence that a prompt experiment is plausible and model-specific, not evidence that DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B must behave the same way as their larger checkpoints. Their binary abstention definition and unanswerable questions differ from SimpleQA's three-class factuality task. Code: `https://github.com/facebookresearch/AbstentionBench` (source tree inspected for annotation resources; not cloned because the full suite was pruned).

**3. Zhang, Hanning; Diao, Shizhe; Lin, Yong; Fung, Yi R.; Lian, Qing; Wang, Xingyao; Chen, Yangyi; Ji, Heng; Zhang, Tong (2023; revised 2024). R-Tuning: Instructing Large Language Models to Say ‘I Don’t Know’.** arXiv:2311.09677v3. Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09677`. Local: `papers/2311.09677_r_tuning.pdf`.

Identifies known/unknown training questions by model-answer agreement, then teaches uncertainty alongside answers. An unsupervised variant uses consistency across sampled answers. Nine datasets include ParaRel, MMLU, HotpotQA, HaluEval, SelfAware, FalseQA, NEC, WiCE and FEVER. Baselines are original pretrained checkpoints, those checkpoints evaluated on willingly answered items, and vanilla instruction tuning; models include OpenLLaMA-3B and LLaMA-7B/13B. Uses answered-subset accuracy and average precision. Table 1 gives LLaMA-13B out-of-domain ParaRel AP 77.30 versus 64.12 for vanilla tuning; not every model/task improves. This motivates measuring both selection quality and answer coverage. It does **not** establish that a one-sentence inference intervention will reproduce training effects. Code: `https://github.com/shizhediao/R-Tuning` (not cloned; training excluded).

**4. Kadavath, Saurav, et al. (2022). Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know.** arXiv:2207.05221v4. Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221`. Local: `papers/2207.05221_know_what_they_know.pdf`.

Studies self-evaluated P(True) of proposed answers and trained P(IK) of knowing an answer. Multiple-choice/true-false benchmarks and generative tasks include TriviaQA, Lambada, GSM8K, arithmetic and code. Compares size, few-shot format, sample context and trained knowledge predictors; evaluates calibration and discrimination, including AUROC. Larger models and appropriate formats improve self-evaluation, while P(IK) generalization remains imperfectly calibrated out of distribution. This supplies a mechanism for selective answering but neither proves introspection nor guarantees that a literal uncertainty instruction induces calibrated decisions. Public complete training/checkpoint reproduction was not established in this resource search. Confidence elicitation is excluded as an additional treatment.

**5. Haas, Lukas; Yona, Gal; D’Antonio, Giovanni; Goldshtein, Sasha; Das, Dipanjan (2025; revised March 10, 2026). SimpleQA Verified: A Reliable Factuality Benchmark to Measure Parametric Knowledge.** arXiv:2509.07968v2. Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07968`. Local: `papers/2509.07968_simpleqa_verified.pdf`.

Produces 1,000 prompts by deduplication, balancing, source checks, ambiguity removal and difficulty filtering. Retains the three-way grading/F1 framework while refining the autorater for elaborate answers, approximate numeric responses and soft refusals. Reviews disagreement-prone outputs obtained from ten high-temperature grader repetitions. The abstract reports Gemini-2.5-Pro F1 55.6 on this new benchmark; this is not comparable to original SimpleQA scores. Filtering also considers publisher training restrictions and frontier-model difficulty, so the subset changes more than label noise alone. Data/evaluation release: `https://www.kaggle.com/benchmarks/deepmind/simpleqa-verified`; Inspect has a separate task. This motivates explicit label-error and judge-domain-shift limitations. We do not substitute Verified or add another dataset experiment.

## Synthesis and experiment recommendations

The papers distinguish three approaches: direct permission to abstain (our treatment), post-generation confidence filtering (Kadavath), and training to express uncertainty (R-Tuning). They also distinguish an answerable question the model does not know (SimpleQA) from an intrinsically unanswerable or underspecified question (AbstentionBench). These distinctions rule out interpreting our abstention rate as a safety-refusal metric.

Use the **raw-question reference baseline** confirmed in both cloned implementations and append only the fixed suffix. Historical published scores are context, not target baselines. No retrieved evidence resolves the size of this intervention's effect on the three specified checkpoints. The study's value is the paired effect and its correct-answer retention, not demonstrating that abstention can trivially lower error by suppressing all answers.

Report correct C/N, incorrect I/N and not-attempted A/N; coverage (C+I)/N; attempted accuracy C/(C+I); and native F = 2C/(2C+2I+A) for fully scored data. Invalid/unscored cases have their own fields and remain in the main N=400 denominator after averaging three seeds per item. Explicit literal abstention should additionally be tracked separately from semantic NOT_ATTEMPTED. A confidence interval crossing the retention margin is inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence.

Synthetic calibration checks that each judge recognizes gold, negation and abstention. It does not test accuracy on the natural distribution of hedges, partial answers and hallucinations. No task-matched public human-labelled model-completion calibration set was located in the reviewed simple-evals/Inspect files or the inspected AbstentionBench source-tree paths; that limited search is not proof none exists. The missing shared `evals/judge-calibration.md` supplies no evidence. Require the staged eligibility thresholds and report the domain-shift caveat even when both judges pass.

Frozen design details, failure gates, seed handling and bounds are in `preregistration.md`; resource acquisition details are in `resources.md`. Preserve the three-direction scope recorded in `planning.md`.
